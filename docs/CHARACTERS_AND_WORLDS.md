# Characters & Worlds (Draft v1)

All names, places and details are placeholders for you to change. Consistent with `GAME_DESIGN.md`: 3 active slots, bench of 1, fixed world order, companions join in World 1's tutorial.

## Theory of magic across worlds
Each world believes something different about where magic comes from. Each belief is *partly true*, and together they point to the Core.

| World | Local belief | Hidden truth it points to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Magic is a **living force** in nature | Magic flows along paths ("veins") that converge |
| 2 | Magic is a **debt** owed and repaid | Every spell draws from something shared, and draws down |
| 3 | Magic is **stored memory** | The source holds the memories/lives of things that came before |
| Final | Magic is a **heart that beats** | The Core: what it is, what happens if it stops |

## Party

### 1. Aruna Valemar, the Mage (protagonist)
- **Cultural inspiration:** a blend of European and South Asian elements, drawn loosely from old-god mythology (e.g. Vedic, Norse and Greco-Roman myth). Think a robe and jewellery mixing both traditions, star and sun motifs, and spells named after ancient deities or their epithets. Her element skills feel like invoking "old gods" of storm, fire, earth and water in fantasy forms, not real deities.
- **Role:** ranged elemental damage, weakness hunter. Gains a new element per world.
- **Personality:** curious, methodical, a little socially clumsy. Keeps a notebook of every spell she sees.
- **Motivation:** her home academy's magic has started to fail in small ways, and nobody will admit it.
- **Arc:** from "knowledge is always good" to understanding its cost.
- **Story world:** World 1.

### 2. Ayo Quispe, the warrior/rogue (hybrid: Duelist)
- **Cultural inspiration:** South American ancestry (e.g. Andean and Afro-Latin influences) combined with partly West African elements. Visual ideas: layered woven textiles, patterned cloth, and bead or brass jewellery. His fighting style is fluid and rhythmic, inspired by martial-dance traditions, with music-like timing for combos.
- **Role:** fast physical damage, many-hit skills for breaking shields, steal/counter utility.
- **Personality:** wry, guarded, distrusts magic. Hides reasons for joining the Mage.
- **Joins:** World 1 tutorial (hires on as a guide, secretly following his own lead).
- **Story world:** World 3 (his lost past explained there).

### 3. Serafina Vidal, the healer/tank (hybrid: Warden)
- **Cultural inspiration:** an **elf** with Spanish influence (Castilian and Andalusian: ornate armour, capes, lace, tile patterns, guitar-like music). Elven grace plus a duelling-knight, bullfighter-cape feel; her shield and cape are the core of her kit.
- **Role:** sturdy frontline, shields and heals, draws enemy attacks.
- **Personality:** warm, stubborn, carries a debt of honour.
- **Joins:** World 1 tutorial (bound to the Mage by an oath, a debt for saving her).
- **Story world:** World 2. **Captured** during her quest.

### 4. The Substitute: Kael Dravin (NPC who joins, bench)
- **Who:** an important figure from Serafina's story: her former rival/mentor in World 2's order (e.g. a disgraced knight-healer).
- **Role:** bruiser/healer hybrid. Heals by hitting: lifesteal strikes, healing burst when an enemy is broken. Different flavour from Serafina's shielding.
- **Why he joins:** he alone knows where Serafina is held and owes her a debt of his own.
- **After rescue:** stays as the 4th roster member; Serafina returns to her slot.

## Worlds

### World 1: Verdance (the Mage's story) — "magic is alive"
- **Biome:** forests, ancient groves, a sprawling academy town. Shrines and carvings to forgotten gods blend European and South Asian architecture, tying to Aruna's roots.
- **Tutorial (opening):** Aruna investigates a failing ward; Ayo and Serafina join; first fights teach turn order, weaknesses, Break, Boost.
- **Conflict:** the groves are withering as the local magic dims. The Mage traces it to a buried vein.
- **Fragment of truth:** magic moves along veins that converge somewhere far away.
- **Unlocks:** Aruna's first new element; the gate to World 2.
- **Bosses (examples):** a warden beast guarding the vein; a rival scholar hoarding the source.

### World 2: Ashmarch (Serafina's story) — "magic is a debt"
- **Biome:** a trading empire of dry cliffs, mines, forges, merchant houses, with Spanish-flavoured architecture (arches, courtyards, tiled palaces) and an elven nobility.
- **Conflict:** mages borrow power from a guild (the Ledger) and repay with years of their lives. Serafina's oath ties to a debt the Ledger exploits.
- **Mid-chapter twist:** Serafina is **captured** by the Ledger. Kael joins to get her back.
- **Climax:** rescue, break the guild master's hold.
- **Fragment of truth:** magic is finite and drawn from a common pool.
- **Unlocks:** Kael (bench), second Mage element, gate to World 3.

### World 3: Hollowmere (Ayo's story) — "magic is memory"
- **Biome:** drowned ruins, mist lakes, a city of archivists and ghosts. The ruins echo Andean terraces and West African clay and stone architecture, matching Ayo's heritage.
- **Conflict:** Ayo's past is tied to a fallen city whose memories are stored in magic. Enemies are echoes of the past.
- **Fragment of truth:** the pool is made of the lives/memories of the past; using magic consumes them.
- **Unlocks:** Ayo's final skills, third Mage element, gate to the final world.

### Final World: The Heartwell (the truth plays out)
- **Biome:** a space where all worlds' landscapes overlap around a glowing Core.
- **What the Core is:** the shared heart of all worlds. It feeds every world's magic, and it is wearing out.
- **Conflict:** the Mage has collected all fragments. The final choice is what to do with the truth.
- **Endings (proposal):** *Protect* (ration magic, cost to everyone), *Release* (end magic, worlds survive without it), *Merge* (Aruna becomes part of the Core, renewing it).
- **Final boss:** a manifestation of the Core's defenses, using elements from every world.

## Cultural approach
- Everything is **fantasy inspired by** real cultures, not a depiction of them. Use original names, invented gods and invented cultures, and avoid caricature or sacred symbols used casually.
- Real deities should not appear as characters or enemies; use invented "old gods" drawing on shared mythic themes.
- Consider sensitivity readers or cultural consultants, especially for Ayo's and Aruna's backgrounds.
- Give these characters specific, human personalities first; heritage informs design, not their whole identity.
